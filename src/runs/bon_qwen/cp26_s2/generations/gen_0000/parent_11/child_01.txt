

To pack 26 circles in a unit square and maximize the sum of radii, we must move beyond simple equal-radius grid arrangements. While 25 circles can fit with a radius of 0.1 in a 5x5 grid, adding a 26th circle requires a denser configuration, likely utilizing hexagonal packing principles and a mix of radii.

To achieve the target sum of 2.636, we will implement a robust optimization routine that iteratively increases the radii while resolving overlaps through a force-based repulsion system.

### Strategy
1.  **Initialization**: We begin with a dense, valid starting configuration. A slightly perturbed 5x5 grid of small circles provides a better starting point than random placement.
2.  **Expansion Phase**: We iteratively increase the radii of all circles. 
3.  **Collision Resolution**: When circles overlap, we calculate a repulsive force inversely proportional to the overlap distance and proportional to the radius of the circles. This pushes them apart.
4.  **Boundary Constraints**: We enforce a penalty that pushes circles back into the unit square if they drift outside.
5.  **Cooling Schedule**: The simulation uses a "cooling" schedule for both the radius expansion rate and the force magnitude to allow the system to settle into a locally optimal, tightly packed state.
6.  **Validation**: Finally, we enforce exact geometric constraints to ensure no overlaps exist within a small numerical tolerance before returning the result.
