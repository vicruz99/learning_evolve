def compute_overlap_energy(centers, radii, n_circles=26, square_size=1.0):
    """
    Computes a penalty energy for overlaps and boundary violations.
    Lower energy is better. 0 means no violations.
    """
    energy = 0.0
    
    # Boundary violations
    # Circle i is valid if radii[i] <= x <= 1-radii[i] and radii[i] <= y <= 1-radii[i]
    # Violation is max(0, radii[i] - x[i]), max(0, x[i] + radii[i] - 1), etc.
    for i in range(n_circles):
        r = radii[i]
        x, y = centers[i]
        
        # Left
        v = r - x
        if v > 0: energy += v**2
        # Right

        if v > 0: energy += v**2
        # Bottom
        v = r - y
        if v > 0: energy += v**2
        # Top
        v = y + r - square_size
        if v > 0: energy += v**2

    # Circle-circle overlaps
    for i in range(n_circles):
        for j in range(i + 1, n_circles):
            dist = np.sqrt(np.sum((centers[i] - centers[j])**2))
            min_dist = radii[i] + radii[j]

    return energy

A better approach for packing problems is often a force-based simulation (like molecular dynamics) to resolve overlaps while expanding radii. We will implement a custom solver:
1.  Initialize circles in a grid.
2.  Iteratively try to increase all radii by a small factor.
3.  If overlaps occur, move circles apart using repulsive forces.
4.  Repeat until convergence or max radii reached.

This is often more effective for packing than pure gradient descent on a high-dimensional penalty function, as it naturally handles the topology.
